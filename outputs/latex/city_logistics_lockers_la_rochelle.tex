\begin{abstract}
Automated parcel lockers (APLs) are widely deployed to relieve the externalities
of e-commerce last-mile delivery, yet their contribution to the wider urban freight
system is decided less by the technology than by \emph{where} lockers are placed.
This paper asks how locker siting reshapes the local urban logistics scheme of a
mid-sized, geographically peripheral city. We pair a state-of-the-art review,
grounded in two professional freight diagnostics of La Rochelle (France), with a
real, multi-year experimentation drawn from three engineering field projects
(2021--2026). A reproducible GIS multicriteria workflow, applied to 148 surveyed
collection points, the public-transport network and population data, yields ten
public-transport-anchored locker sites and exposes a coverage gap between locker
supply and demand density. A companion land survey classifies 95~public car parks
by goods-vehicle compatibility and identifies 74~surface sites suitable for
micro-hub installation, showing that the land needed to anchor consolidation near
demand is already available. Coupling locker siting with this consolidation
foncier and cyclo-logistics produces an estimated 40\% CO$_2$ reduction over five
years and a 44\% cut in truck-kilometres on a real carrier perimeter. We conclude
that, for ``end-of-line'' cities, locker networks deliver system value only when
co-designed with consolidation rather than deployed as stand-alone retail amenities.
\end{abstract}

\textbf{Keywords:} city logistics; automated parcel lockers; last-mile delivery;
facility location; collection-and-delivery points; e-commerce; La Rochelle.

\section{Introduction and Objective}

E-commerce has pushed B2C parcel flows into city centres faster than urban freight
systems have adapted. Automated parcel lockers answer part of this problem: as
unattended, out-of-home collection-and-delivery points, they consolidate many drops
into one secured stop and let recipients collect along trips they already make.

The value a locker network returns to the system, however, is fixed at the siting
stage. A locker placed for real-estate convenience adds an asset without changing
flows; one placed where demand, accessibility and the carrier network coincide
restructures rounds, shifts collection out of the home, and creates the volume
that makes consolidated, low-emission distribution viable. Evidence on this
siting--impact link comes mostly from large metropolitan areas. Mid-sized,
``end-of-line'' cities remain thinly documented, even though they face the same
regulatory pressure to decarbonise.

This paper addresses that gap: \textbf{how does locker siting reshape the local
urban logistics scheme of a mid-sized city?} We answer it for La Rochelle (France),
a coastal agglomeration of $\approx$170,000 inhabitants targeting carbon neutrality
by 2040, with regulated city-centre delivery windows (06:00--11:00) and
$\approx$14,000 students. The contribution is twofold: a transferable, data-driven
siting method, and evidence that PT-anchored siting combined with consolidation
reshapes --- not merely supplements --- the local scheme.

\section{State of the Art and Local Context}

\subsection{Locker Siting as a City-Logistics Scheme}

Locker location is a facility-location problem balancing coverage, demand density
and multimodal accessibility against capital cost and the cannibalisation of
existing relay points. The literature converges on a compact set of siting criteria:
proximity to PT nodes and soft-mode networks, residential and footfall density, and
insertion into daily mobility chains. Reported effects include re-delivery reductions
of roughly 30\% and markedly lower per-parcel emissions than van home delivery
\citep{iwan2016,deutsch2018,lachapelle2018}. PT-anchored pilots (Valencia, SPROUT;
Paris, RATP--Pickup) concentrate carrier drops at one node while travellers collect
en route.

\subsection{The La Rochelle Freight System}

Two professional diagnostics frame the local scheme. The 2018 FRETURB-based study
quantified about \textbf{101,300 goods movements per week} across the agglomeration,
of which roughly \textbf{2,290~per day occur in the historic centre}; e-commerce
reached $\approx$\textbf{0.31~parcels/household/week} ($\approx$25,300~weekly
deliveries, near 20\% of all movements), concentrated on two poles: the dense
historic core (Centre~Marché, $\approx$510~parcels/week) and the social-housing
district of Mireuil ($\approx$285~parcels/week) \citep{it2018}. The 2024
logistics-real-estate study characterised La Rochelle as an ``end-of-line'' market
with few resident national operators and proposed a hierarchical typology of
logistics objects \citep{it2024}.

\section{Case Study and Method}

The experimentation rests on three EIGSI field projects:

\textbf{P1 (2021) -- Cargo-bike last mile.} Hyper-centre flow analysis (633~shops,
38~streets, $\approx$31\% pedestrian zones); comparative CO$_2$ accounting
(cargo-bike $\approx$13~g/km vs.\ thermal van $\approx$220~g/km) \citep{eigsi2021}.

\textbf{P2 (2024) -- Locker and micro-hub siting.} A reproducible GIS
multicriteria workflow (Python, GeoPandas, Folium): (1)~inventory of
148~existing collection points (120~relay points/agencies + 9~APLs); (2)~enrichment
with PT stops (Yélo, 1,414~stops), bike-share and car parks; (3)~five-minute
walking buffers; (4)~multicriteria ranking: PT proximity $>$ non-cannibalisation $>$
population/footfall density. Output: \textbf{ten PT-anchored priority sites}
\citep{eigsi2024}.

\textbf{P3 (2025--26) -- VerDelivery + foncier survey.} Consolidation scenario
(OVO model): peripheral nano-hubs in car parks, light-goods-vehicle supply +
cargo-bike final leg, assessed on a real carrier (Aunis Messagerie). Companion
survey: 95~public car parks classified by LGV compatibility (Fig.~\ref{fig:map})
\citep{eigsi2026}.

\section{Results}

\textbf{Coverage gap.} Only 9~APLs versus 120~relay points; lockers cluster in the
commercial centre and weakly align with the densest residential quartiers. The
1,414-stop Yélo network provides dense candidate anchors across the demand surface.

\textbf{Siting.} Ten PT-integrated, high-footfall, under-served candidate sites
(railway station, Place de Verdun, university campus, Yélo interchanges), each
complementary to existing relays and micro-hub candidates. PT-first ranking keeps
the network legible and travel-chain-aligned.

\textbf{Locker benchmarks.} $\approx$6,200~parcels/year per unit; PT-station
lockers (Valencia) saved $\approx$3,937~kgCO$_2$/year each; $\approx$12~g
CO$_2$/parcel vs.\ $\approx$50~g for van delivery.

\textbf{Land for consolidation is available.} The foncier survey (Fig.~\ref{fig:map})
classifies 74~surface car parks as micro-hub candidates ($\geq$3~m clearance),
9~conditional, 12~structured/excluded. Candidates spread from centre to periphery.
The Espace Encan car park ($\approx$420~spaces) was selected as the VerDelivery
nano-hub pilot. The ``no land'' objection to urban consolidation does not hold here:
the binding constraint is governance, not foncier.

\textbf{System-level impact.} Coupling locker siting with nano-hub consolidation
and cargo-bike distribution yields: $\approx$\textbf{40\% CO$_2$ reduction} over
five years (236 $\to$ 128~tCO$_2$e); \textbf{44\% cut in truck-kilometres};
$\approx$\textbf{\euro{}5,505/year} in fuel savings on the Aunis Messagerie
perimeter \citep{eigsi2026}. Lockers act as demand anchors that pull dispersed home
deliveries into consolidated, off-peak provisioning and create the volume that
justifies cargo-bike distribution.

\section{Discussion}

\textbf{Siting is the decisive lever.} PT-anchored, density-aware placement
maximises utilisation and modal-shift potential. Sites chosen for availability alone
risk cannibalising relay points ($\approx$30\% of host-shop turnover) without net
system gain. Real barriers persist: home-delivery preference, insufficient network
density, capital cost (\euro{}1,000/year installed; \euro{}3,000--30,000/unit),
PMR accessibility, and governance requiring public--private coordination.

The central lesson for mid-sized ``end-of-line'' cities is \textbf{integration}.
A locker network returns system-level benefits only when co-designed with
consolidation (nano-/micro-hubs) and local cyclo-logistics. The foncier survey
reframes the usual objection: with 74~compatible car parks, the land is available;
the constraint is institutional, not spatial.

%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Conclusion and Perspectives}

This work connects locker siting to system-level impact through two professional
diagnostics and three years of field experimentation. It offers a transferable GIS
siting workflow and evidence that PT-anchored siting plus consolidation
\emph{reshapes} the local scheme rather than merely adding capacity.

Planned extensions: capacitated coverage / p-median optimisation of the ten
candidate sites; post-deployment utilisation monitoring; full-territory simulation;
integration into the agglomeration's logistics roadmap and \textit{Schéma Directeur
des Aires de Livraison}.
