\title{From convenience asset to network lever: siting automated parcel
lockers and its impact on the local urban logistics scheme --- the case of
La Rochelle (France)}

\author[eigsi]{Corwin Fevre}
\author[eigsi]{Tatiana Graindorge}
\author[eigsi]{Robin David}
\author[eigsi]{Jean-Philippe Samier}
\address[eigsi]{EIGSI La Rochelle, 26 rue Vaux de Foletier, 17041 La Rochelle, France}

\begin{abstract}
Automated parcel lockers (APLs) are increasingly promoted as a city-logistics
scheme to mitigate the externalities of e-commerce last-mile delivery. Their
effectiveness, however, depends critically on \emph{where they are sited}. This
paper examines how locker location decisions reshape the \emph{local} urban
logistics scheme, using La Rochelle (France) --- a mid-sized Atlantic city
pursuing carbon neutrality by 2040 --- as a case study. We combine (i) a
state-of-the-art review of locker siting and last-mile consolidation, grounded
in two professional freight diagnostics of the territory (2018, 2024), and
(ii) a real-world experimentation drawing on three field-based engineering
projects (2021--2026) that mapped relay points, applied multicriteria GIS
analysis to propose ten locker sites anchored to the public-transport network,
and quantified the effects of coupling lockers with micro-/nano-hubs and
cyclo-logistics. Results indicate that demand- and accessibility-driven siting,
integrated with upstream consolidation, can cut last-mile vehicle movements and
CO\textsubscript{2} while improving service quality, and that mid-sized
``end-of-line'' cities require locker networks co-designed with local
consolidation rather than stand-alone deployments.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The growth of e-commerce has intensified business-to-consumer (B2C) parcel
flows in city centres, amplifying the externalities of the last mile:
congestion, local emissions, noise, kerbside competition and failed
first-attempt deliveries. Automated parcel lockers --- out-of-home, unattended
collection-and-delivery points (CDPs) --- are now a mainstream city-logistics
scheme: by consolidating many drops into a single secured stop with 24/7 access
they reduce delivery rounds and failed deliveries while shifting the final leg
to the consumer's existing mobility patterns.

The benefit of a locker network, however, is largely determined at the
\emph{siting} stage. A poorly located locker is under-used; a well-located one
captures latent demand, integrates with public transport (PT) and soft modes,
and restructures local flows. Evidence is dominated by large metropolitan areas,
while mid-sized, ``end-of-line'' cities --- peripheral to national corridors and
reliant on local subcontractors --- remain under-studied. The \textbf{objective}
of this paper is to assess how locker siting reshapes the local urban logistics
scheme of such a city, La Rochelle, combining a structured state of the art with
a multi-year field experimentation. La Rochelle targets carbon neutrality by
2040, regulates city-centre access (deliveries 06:00--11:00; objective of 100\%
low-emission delivery vehicles), hosts $\sim$14{,}000 students and has a dense,
constrained historic core.

\section{State of the art and local context}
\subsection{Locker siting as a city-logistics lever}
Locker location is a facility-location problem balancing coverage, demand
density and multimodal accessibility against capital cost and cannibalisation of
existing CDPs. The literature converges on a small set of siting criteria ---
proximity to PT nodes and soft-mode networks, residential and footfall density,
and integration into daily mobility chains --- and reports that out-of-home
delivery can cut re-deliveries by $\sim$30\% and lower per-parcel emissions
relative to van home delivery [verify]. PT-anchored deployments (e.g.\ Valencia,
SPROUT; Paris, RATP--Pickup) let travellers collect parcels along existing
trips, concentrating carrier drops at a single node.

\subsection{The La Rochelle freight system}
Two professional diagnostics frame the local scheme. The 2018 FRETURB study
quantified $\sim$101{,}300 goods movements/week across the agglomeration, of
which $\sim$2{,}290/day occur in the city centre; e-commerce was estimated at
0.31 parcels/household/week ($\sim$25{,}300 deliveries/week, $\sim$20\% of all
movements). The territory relied on an electric urban consolidation centre
(Elcidis, 2001--2018) and on 72 city-centre delivery bays, $\sim$80\% undersized.
The 2024 logistics-real-estate study added an object typology (urban delivery \&
service spaces --- including lockers; urban distribution spaces; urban
distribution platforms; regional gateways) and characterised La Rochelle as an
``end-of-line'' market with few national operators. Together these establish both
the demand for out-of-home delivery and the constraints any locker network must
respect.

\section{Case study and method}
The experimentation draws on three EIGSI--CdA field projects:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{P1 (2021) --- Cargo-bike last mile.} Hyper-centre flow analysis
  (633 shops, 38 streets, $\sim$31\% in pedestrian zones); comparative CO\textsubscript{2}
  accounting (cargo-bike $\approx$13 g/km vs.\ thermal van $\approx$220 g/km).
  \item \textbf{P2 (2024) --- Locker \& micro-hub siting.} A reproducible
  multicriteria GIS workflow (Python/GeoPandas/Folium): inventory of relay
  points/lockers; enrichment with PT stops (Y\'elo), bike-share and car parks;
  5-minute walking buffers; multicriteria selection (PT proximity first,
  non-cannibalisation of relays, demand/footfall density including students).
  Output: \textbf{ten priority locker sites}, each anchored to a bus stop.
  \item \textbf{P3 (2025--2026) --- VerDelivery.} A consolidation scenario (OVO
  model): peripheral car-park \emph{nano-hubs} fed by light goods vehicles, with
  cargo-bikes performing the final leg; assessed on the Aunis Messagerie perimeter.
\end{itemize}
Impacts are evaluated with per-mode CO\textsubscript{2} factors,
vehicle-movement and vehicle-kilometre reductions, and a consolidation logic
linking siting to the wider scheme.

\section{Results}
\textbf{A visible coverage gap.} Mapping the surveyed network (Fig.~\ref{fig:map})
shows 120 relay points but only 9 automated lockers, clustered along the centre
and the eastern commercial axis and weakly aligned with the densest residential
quartiers identified in 2018; the Y\'elo network instead offers 1{,}414 candidate
anchor nodes across the demand surface.
\textbf{Siting.} The analysis returns ten PT-integrated, high-footfall,
under-served candidate locations (railway station, Place de Verdun, university
residences/campus, Y\'elo interchanges), complementary to existing relays and
candidate micro-hubs.

\textbf{Locker benchmarks (corpus).} 24/7 access and fewer failed deliveries;
$\sim$6{,}200 parcels/yr per unit; PT-station lockers in the Valencia pilot saved
$\approx$3{,}937 kg CO\textsubscript{2}/yr each; $\sim$12 g CO\textsubscript{2}/parcel
vs.\ $\sim$50 g for a van delivery.

\textbf{System-level impact.} Coupling siting with consolidation and
cyclo-logistics (P3) yields $\approx$\textbf{$-$40\% CO\textsubscript{2} over five
years} (236$\to$128 tCO\textsubscript{2}e), \textbf{$-$44\% truck-km}, and
$\sim$\euro 5{,}505/yr fuel savings on the studied perimeter. Lockers act as
\emph{demand anchors}, pulling B2C drops out of dispersed home-delivery rounds
into off-peak, consolidated provisioning and justifying micro-/nano-hub +
cargo-bike distribution. Anchoring lockers to the Y\'elo network materialises a
passenger--freight interface.

\section{Discussion}
Siting is the decisive lever: PT-anchored, density-driven placement maximises
utilisation and modal-shift potential, whereas sites that cannibalise relay
points erode a revenue stream ($\sim$30\% of host-shop turnover) without net
gain. Barriers include a persistent home-delivery preference, insufficient
network density, capital cost ($\approx$\euro 1{,}000/yr installed; \euro 3--30k
per unit), accessibility for reduced-mobility users and public--private
governance. For mid-sized ``end-of-line'' cities the lesson is integration: a
locker network delivers system benefits only when co-designed with consolidation
and local cyclo-logistics, reframing lockers from a convenience product into a
structuring component of the local scheme.

\section{Conclusion and perspectives}
The paper links locker \emph{siting} to \emph{system-level impact} in a real
mid-sized case, bridging professional diagnostics and field experimentation. It
contributes a transferable GIS-based multicriteria siting workflow and evidence
that PT-anchored siting plus consolidation reshapes the local scheme.
Perspectives: a capacitated coverage/p-median model, post-deployment utilisation
monitoring, full-territory simulation, and integration into the CdA logistics
roadmap and \emph{Sch\'ema Directeur des Aires de Livraison}.
