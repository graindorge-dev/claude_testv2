## Working title
*Rethinking Evaluation: How Generative AI Is Reshaping Assessment Practices in Higher Education*

## Research question
How is the adoption of generative AI tools (e.g., large language models) changing the design, delivery, and integrity of student assessment in higher education, and what does this imply for institutional policy?

## Thesis (provisional)
Generative AI does not merely threaten traditional assessment; it forces a shift from **product-based** evaluation (grading a final artifact) toward **process- and competency-based** evaluation, requiring new assessment designs, integrity frameworks, and faculty support.

---

## Chapter 1 — Context and Problem Statement
**Goal:** Establish why generative AI is a turning point for assessment.

- 1.1 The rapid diffusion of generative AI tools among students and faculty.
- 1.2 What "assessment" traditionally measures, and where it is vulnerable (take-home essays, coding tasks, problem sets).
- 1.3 The integrity gap: detection tools are unreliable; bans are hard to enforce.
- 1.4 Research question, scope, and contribution of the paper.

**Evidence to gather:** adoption surveys, institutional policy statements, detector-accuracy studies.

## Chapter 2 — How Assessment Practices Are Changing
**Goal:** Map the concrete shifts in assessment design and practice.

- 2.1 From product to process: drafts, oral defenses, reflective logs, version histories.
- 2.2 Authentic and competency-based assessment as AI-resilient designs.
- 2.3 "AI-permitted" vs. "AI-restricted" assessment: disclosure and citation of AI use.
- 2.4 Faculty workload, equity, and access concerns.

**Evidence to gather:** case studies, redesign frameworks, equity/access literature.

## Chapter 3 — Implications and a Framework for Institutions
**Goal:** Move from observation to actionable guidance.

- 3.1 A decision framework: when to restrict, permit, or require AI use in an assessment.
- 3.2 Policy levers: academic-integrity codes, disclosure norms, faculty development.
- 3.3 Limitations of current evidence and a research agenda.
- 3.4 Conclusion: assessment as a measure of learning, not of artifact production.

**Evidence to gather:** existing institutional frameworks, policy analyses, validity/reliability theory.
